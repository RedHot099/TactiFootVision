Expected goals from broadcast video

Jakub Eichner · TactiFoot Vision · research summary, October 2026

Expected-goals (xG) models estimate the probability that a shot becomes a goal. Commercial xG relies on event data that human analysts annotate by hand, including the positions of every player at the moment of the shot. This project asks how much of that information a model can recover directly from broadcast video, and where it still falls short. It combines an end-to-end video model with an explicit perception stack (player and ball detection, pitch registration) and compares both against StatsBomb's commercial xG in a pre-registered study.

2.1 A video-only xG model

A DINOv2-L backbone, with its last 4 of 24 transformer blocks fine-tuned, reads a single frame at the moment of the shot and outputs a calibrated goal probability. Ten models (5 folds grouped by home team × 2 seeds) are averaged, then Platt-scaled. On the official SoccerNet test split (100 matches, 2,229 shots) it reaches the following:

Metric (test, 2,229 shots)Video-only modelConstant baselineNote

A larger ensemble with other architectures was no better (ΔAUC +0.001 [−0.003, +0.005]), so the simpler model is kept. Robustness: training without one league and testing on it costs only +0.004 log-loss (1.2%) [0.002, 0.006] across the five domestic leagues. The conclusions hold under alternative rules for attributing goals to rebound shots.

2.2 Video versus commercial event data (pre-registered)

I paired 2,754 SoccerNet shots one-to-one with StatsBomb Open Data events (135 matches, 99.5% goal agreement). The hypotheses, margins and analysis were registered before the labels were joined. On the 2,721 non-penalty shots, video alone reaches a log-loss of 0.355 (AUC 0.736), and StatsBomb xG 0.326 (AUC 0.792). The video model still trails manually annotated event data. The registered tests explain why:

  • The frame adds nothing beyond hand-annotated player positions. Adding the video model to a model on StatsBomb's annotated positions changes log-loss by −0.0004 [−0.0026, +0.0018]. That is equivalent within the pre-set margin δ = 0.005 (TOST p = 0.0006).
  • The video model has learned shot geometry implicitly. Its embedding linearly encodes the distance to goal (R² = 0.71) and the number of defenders in the shooting cone (AUC 0.76). Both are above the pre-registered thresholds and above frozen DINOv2 features.

Against hand-annotated positions, the useful content of the frame is mostly geometry. That points at the perception stack: measure the geometry explicitly and accurately. Section 2.5 asks the converse question for automatically measured geometry. A paper on this study is in preparation for the CVsports workshop at CVPR 2027.

2.5 What does the image add beyond automatic geometry?

A three-arm ablation, out of fold on the 328 training matches (7,062 shots, 902 goals), with identical folds and calibration and paired match-bootstrap CIs. These are development results on a different population from 2.1.

ArmInputsLog-lossBrierAUC

  • Each adds to the other. Image beyond geometry (C−B): −0.0097 [−0.0131, −0.0064]. Geometry beyond image (C−A): −0.0106 [−0.0143, −0.0065]. B−A is undecided. Leakage controls agree. The model keeps both (arm C).
  • Not a geometry correction. Probes read distance, angle, defenders in the cone and goalkeeper depth (mostly in blocks 22–23 of 24), but the detector is closer to StatsBomb's reference positions than any probe.
  • Not simple scene attributes. Crowding, set piece, framing and shooter size, added to geometry, leave the gain at −0.0097 [−0.0136, −0.0057] (inconclusive: the test can confirm redundancy, not exclude it). No detectable difference for headers or set pieces.
